# Purpose

Identity Forge exists to reconstruct historical people—not reinvent them.

The purpose of this document is to define the methodology by which Identity Forge transforms incomplete, inconsistent, or imperfect historical references into a single canonical character suitable for cinematic storytelling.

This document establishes the principles that govern historical reconstruction before any stylization, colorization, rendering, or artistic interpretation occurs.

The reconstruction process is evidence-driven, historically respectful, and intentionally conservative. Artistic interpretation is permitted only after the person's identity has been reconstructed as faithfully as possible.

Identity Forge is not attempting to answer the question:

> "What would make the coolest character?"

It is answering a different question:

> "Based on the best available historical evidence, who was this person, and how can we faithfully introduce them to a modern child?"

That introduction should preserve not only appearance,

but the human reality of uncertainty, discipline, sacrifice, persistence, and earned achievement when the evidence supports it.

---

# Core Philosophy

Historical references are not photographs.

Most historical figures lived before photography. Their likenesses survive through paintings, sculptures, engravings, manuscripts, coins, mosaics, written descriptions, and modern academic reconstructions.

Each source contains two different kinds of information:

* Information about the person.
* Information about the artist or medium.

Identity Forge must separate these.

The objective is always to preserve the first while removing the second.

Historical reconstruction should also preserve evidence of the person's lived conditions:

what they struggled against,

what they continued through,

and what their work made possible.

# Evidence Hierarchy

Not all historical references are equally reliable.

Identity Forge assigns every source a confidence level.

## Tier 1 — Direct Physical Evidence

Highest confidence.

Examples:

- Photographs
- Multiple authenticated photographs
- Motion picture footage
- Life masks
- Death masks
- High-resolution facial scans of preserved remains

Whenever Tier 1 evidence exists, it becomes the primary identity reference.

---

## Tier 2 — Contemporary Representation

Created during the person's lifetime.

Examples:

- Portraits commissioned from life
- Sculptures created from observation
- Drawings made by contemporaries
- Painted portraits approved by the subject

These remain subject to artistic interpretation but are considered highly valuable.

---

## Tier 3 — Near-Contemporary Sources

Created shortly after death.

Examples:

- Early memorial portraits
- Sculptures based upon direct witnesses
- Family commissions
- Contemporary copies

These often preserve facial identity while introducing stylistic interpretation.

---

## Tier 4 — Historical Artistic Sources

Examples:

- Oil paintings
- Frescoes
- Engravings
- Woodcuts
- Illustrated manuscripts
- Coins
- Medallions
- Relief sculptures

These may preserve identity but often exaggerate style.

They require careful interpretation.

---

## Tier 5 — Academic Reconstruction

Examples:

- Museum reconstructions
- Historical consultants
- Forensic artists
- Archaeological reconstruction
- Modern scholarly illustrations

Useful when primary sources are weak.

---

## Tier 6 — Historical Description

Written evidence.

Examples:

- biographies
- letters
- eyewitness accounts
- diaries
- court records
- travel journals

These often describe:

- height
- posture
- complexion
- beard
- hair
- clothing
- demeanor

Such descriptions help resolve ambiguity.

---

## Tier 7 — Reasoned Inference

Lowest confidence.

Only used when no evidence exists.

Must always be documented.

Never presented as fact.

# Multi-Source Identity Fusion

Almost every important historical figure has conflicting references.

Newton looks different across engravings.

Archimedes appears differently in Renaissance paintings.

Hypatia has dozens of contradictory depictions.

Identity Forge never chooses a single source blindly.

Instead, it performs identity fusion.

The objective is to discover the stable characteristics shared across multiple sources.

---

# Identity Anchors

Identity is reconstructed from recurring traits.

Examples:

- skull proportions
- brow shape
- nose geometry
- eye spacing
- chin
- facial width
- beard silhouette
- hairstyle
- age
- posture
- clothing traditions

Traits appearing consistently across multiple independent sources receive higher confidence.

---

# Source Conflict Resolution

When two references disagree:

Questions are asked in order.

## Which source is older?

Older sources generally receive higher weight.

---

## Which source was created from life?

Observation outranks imagination.

---

## Which source has independent confirmation?

Multiple independent agreements increase confidence.

---

## Which source demonstrates artistic convention?

Some periods exaggerated:

- noses
- eyes
- body proportions
- clothing
- posture

These conventions should be discounted.

---

## Which interpretation best explains all evidence?

Identity Forge favors consensus rather than extremes.

---

# Unknown Attributes

Historical uncertainty must be respected.

Some details simply cannot be known.

Examples include:

- exact eye color
- precise hair color
- exact skin undertone
- height
- weight
- voice
- smile
- handedness

These should never be fabricated with unjustified confidence.

Instead:

```
Known

Likely

Possible

Unknown
```

should accompany reconstruction decisions internally.

Uncertainty is not a flaw in historical storytelling.

It is part of history.

Children should understand that knowledge was often built by people working without certainty,

and continuing anyway.

---

# Historical Authenticity Rules

Identity Forge follows several immutable principles.

## Never Beautify

Historical people are not fashion models.

Do not narrow noses.

Do not enlarge lips.

Do not remove asymmetry.

Do not "Hollywood-ize" faces.

---

## Never Homogenize

Different cultures should remain visibly different.

Regional facial characteristics should be respected.

Avoid producing one generic attractive face for every historical figure.

---

## Never Modernize

Do not introduce:

modern hairstyles

modern eyebrows

modern makeup

modern facial grooming

modern clothing

modern fashion cues

---

## Never Remove Age

Age tells story.

Wrinkles.

Grey hair.

Weathered skin.

Smile lines.

Experience.

These are identity.

They may be softened for animation.

They must not disappear.

# Reconstruction Confidence

Each canonical reconstruction receives one rating.

## Gold

Exceptional evidence.

Multiple photographs or direct references.

Little ambiguity.

---

## Silver

Strong historical evidence.

Minor uncertainty.

Suitable for production.

---

## Bronze

Limited evidence.

Several reasonable interpretations exist.

Requires careful review.

---

## Research Required

Insufficient evidence.

Character cannot yet become canonical.

---

# Failure Modes

The reconstruction process has failed if:

- the character resembles the artist more than the historical figure
- one painting is copied blindly
- engraving artifacts become anatomy
- historical uncertainty is ignored
- evidence hierarchy is skipped
- beauty replaces authenticity
- modern fashion replaces historical appearance
- identity becomes generic

---

# Success Criteria

A successful reconstruction achieves all of the following.

✓ Preserves recognizable historical identity.

✓ Uses the strongest available evidence.

✓ Resolves conflicts logically.

✓ Documents uncertainty honestly.

✓ Produces a believable human being.

✓ Preserves historically supported evidence of effort, uncertainty, and persistence.

✓ Frames achievement as earned rather than inevitable.

✓ Creates a stable canonical identity suitable for long-term production.

✓ Serves as the foundation for every future image, animation, and scene.

---

# Final Principle

Identity Forge is not restoring artwork.

It is restoring people.

Every engraving, painting, sculpture, or manuscript is merely a window through which we glimpse a real human being who once lived.

The responsibility of Identity Forge is to clean that window carefully enough that modern children feel they are finally meeting the person—not the centuries of artistic interpretation that accumulated around them.

That person becomes the canonical character.

Everything else begins there.
