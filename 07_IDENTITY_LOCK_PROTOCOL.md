# Purpose

The purpose of the Identity Lock Protocol is to ensure that every historical figure remains visually recognizable throughout their entire lifecycle.

Identity Forge does not create isolated images.

It creates permanent digital actors.

Every future portrait, illustration, animation frame, expression sheet, cinematic shot, and Director Desk scene must represent the same individual.

Identity Lock prevents identity drift.

---

# Core Philosophy

Children develop relationships with characters.

Those relationships depend upon visual consistency.

If Newton's jaw changes from one illustration to another…

If Marie Curie's eyes suddenly become larger…

If Ibn al-Haytham's beard changes shape every generation…

the illusion breaks.

Identity Forge therefore treats identity as immutable.

The image may change.

The lighting may change.

The camera may change.

The expression may change.

The identity may not.

---

# Identity Before Style

Identity always outranks style.

A beautiful image that loses the historical person's identity is considered a failure.

A technically imperfect image that faithfully preserves identity is far more valuable than a visually impressive impostor.

Every artistic decision must pass this question:

> "Does this strengthen or weaken recognition of the historical person?"

If the answer is "weaken,"

the decision should be rejected.

---

# The Canonical Character

Every historical figure possesses one canonical identity.

This canonical identity becomes the source of truth for every downstream generation.

It is never replaced casually.

Instead, it evolves only through explicit approval and versioning.

The canonical character is the equivalent of casting an actor.

Once cast,

the actor remains the same person throughout the production.

---

# Identity Anchors

Identity is not one feature.

It is a constellation of stable characteristics.

Identity Forge classifies these into three categories.

---

# Primary Identity Anchors

These define the person.

They should remain effectively unchanged across all generations.

Examples:

- skull shape
- facial proportions
- eye spacing
- brow structure
- nose geometry
- jawline
- chin
- ear placement
- forehead
- facial asymmetry
- age category

Primary anchors define recognition.

Changing them creates a different person.

---

# Secondary Identity Anchors

These support recognition.

Examples:

- hairstyle
- beard
- moustache
- hairline
- clothing silhouette
- glasses
- hats
- jewelry
- signature accessories
- posture

Secondary anchors may evolve slightly depending upon story context but should remain historically appropriate.

---

# Tertiary Identity Anchors

These communicate role and personality.

Examples:

- books
- tools
- laboratory equipment
- prism
- telescope
- astrolabe
- notebooks
- drafting instruments
- workshop

These reinforce recognition but do not define identity.

---

# Immutable Characteristics

The following should never change without overwhelming historical evidence.

- face geometry

- eye position

- jaw

- nose

- age

- cultural identity

- ethnicity

- recognizable facial asymmetry

- signature beard style

- historically established hairstyle

- historically documented clothing traditions

Identity Forge protects these features aggressively.

# Identity Drift

Identity drift is the gradual loss of recognizable features across repeated generations.

This is one of the greatest dangers in AI-assisted production.

Small changes accumulate.

Eventually the character becomes someone else.

Identity Forge exists to prevent this.

---

# Common Causes of Identity Drift

Repeated image-to-image generation.

Prompt rewriting.

Model upgrades.

Style transfer.

Excessive denoising.

Over-aggressive refinement.

Different generators.

Different LoRAs.

Multiple artists.

Human editing.

Lossy reference images.

Every generation introduces risk.

---

# Identity Drift Detection

Identity Forge continuously asks:

Would someone familiar with this character immediately recognize them?

If not,

identity drift has occurred.

Questions include:

Has the jaw changed?

Has the eye spacing changed?

Has the nose become generic?

Has age shifted?

Has facial width changed?

Has the hairstyle become inconsistent?

Has the beard evolved into another shape?

Has the character become prettier instead of more accurate?

If yes,

reconstruction should stop.

---

# Identity Recovery

If drift occurs,

Identity Forge returns to the canonical source.

Never attempt to repair identity using already-drifted generations.

Instead:

Canonical Character

↓

Identity Analysis

↓

Difference Detection

↓

Correction

↓

Validation

↓

Resume Generation

Recovery always begins from truth.

# Costume Variation

Historical characters may appear in different clothing.

However:

their wardrobe should remain historically plausible.

Examples:

Newton may wear:

formal robes

study clothing

travel attire

winter clothing

He should never appear in:

modern suits

science-fiction uniforms

fantasy robes

generic wizard clothing

Costume supports identity.

It does not redefine it.

---

# Age Variation

Some productions may depict multiple life stages.

Identity Forge requires continuity.

Young Newton

Adult Newton

Older Newton

should clearly be understood as the same person.

Aging should preserve:

bone structure

eye spacing

nose

jaw

personality

Only age-related characteristics should evolve.

---

# Identity Lock Checklist

Before approving a canonical character ask:

✓ Is the face unmistakably recognizable?

✓ Are the primary identity anchors preserved?

✓ Has stylization remained subordinate to identity?

✓ Would this character remain recognizable across hundreds of generated images?

✓ Could another artist reproduce this character accurately?

✓ Would a child recognize the character immediately after seeing them several times?

✓ Does the character remain faithful to the historical evidence?

If any answer is no,

continue refinement.

---

# The One-Second Recognition Test

Identity Forge adopts a simple production rule.

If the character appears on screen for one second,

a child who has already met them in previous episodes should immediately know who they are.

No narration should be required.

No title card should be required.

Recognition should be instantaneous.

This is the standard.

---

# Relationship to Director Desk

Identity Forge produces the actor.

Director Desk directs the actor.

Identity Forge should never be asked to solve scene continuity.

Director Desk should never redefine character identity.

The canonical package produced by Identity Forge is the single source of truth for every downstream production.

---

# Version Control

Canonical characters are versioned.

Minor improvements:

v1.0

↓

v1.1

↓

v1.2

Major identity changes require explicit approval.

Previous versions remain archived.

Identity history should never be lost.

Every change must be traceable.

---

# Success Criteria

Identity Lock succeeds when:

✓ Every image depicts the same person.

✓ Every scene preserves recognition.

✓ Every refinement strengthens rather than weakens identity.

✓ Historical evidence remains visible.

✓ Stylization never overwhelms authenticity.

✓ Director Desk can reliably generate hundreds of consistent scenes without identity drift.

---

# Final Principle

Identity Forge does not generate pictures.

It casts historical actors.

Once a character enters the Identity Forge Universe, that identity becomes permanent.

Expressions may change.

Lighting may change.

Camera angles may change.

Stories may change.

History may unfold.

But the person remains the same.

Identity is not an output.

Identity is a promise.

That promise is protected by the Identity Lock Protocol.
