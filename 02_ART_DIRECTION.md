## Purpose

This document defines the visual art direction for Identity Forge.

It describes how every canonical character should look and feel.

The goal is a unified cinematic style that blends historical realism with expressive animated appeal.

---

## Core Art Direction Statement

Identity Forge characters should look like historically grounded people transformed into high-end cinematic animated characters for a family adventure film.

They should be:

- recognizable
- expressive
- human
- tactile
- cinematic
- colorful
- physically believable
- emotionally readable
- emotionally authentic
- appealing to children

They should not look like:

- textbook illustrations
- wax figures
- stiff portraits
- generic cartoons
- parody caricatures
- hyperreal uncanny humans
- low-budget educational animation

---

## Rendering Style

The rendering style combines:

- cinematic 3D character design
- realistic skin and material texture
- stylized expressive proportions
- warm volumetric lighting
- rich color
- shallow depth of field
- historical set design
- family-film emotional clarity

The image should feel expensive, polished, and story-ready.

---

## Lighting

Lighting is one of the strongest universe unifiers.

Use:

- soft cinematic key light
- warm highlights
- gentle volumetric atmosphere
- subtle rim light
- shallow depth of field
- natural environmental bounce light
- emotionally focused facial illumination

Avoid:

- flat museum lighting
- harsh horror lighting
- muddy shadows
- overexposed faces
- cold sterile lighting
- random neon lighting
- excessive dramatic darkness

The face must remain readable.

---

## Color

The color palette should be:

- vibrant
- saturated
- warm
- optimistic
- historically plausible
- emotionally inviting

Avoid:

- sepia unless specifically motivated
- black-and-white outputs
- muddy browns
- gray academic palettes
- oversaturated toy plastic colors
- random fantasy colors

Color should help children want to look longer.

---

## Skin

Skin should be realistic but softened for animated appeal.

Use:

- subsurface scattering
- gentle pores
- soft warmth
- natural variation
- believable age texture
- expressive cheeks and eyes

Avoid:

- waxy skin
- plastic skin
- overly wrinkled harsh realism
- dead statue texture
- oil painting texture
- engraving texture
- uncanny hyperreal skin

Age should be respected, but visual harshness should be softened.

---

## Eyes

Eyes are the emotional center.

Use:

- slightly enlarged expressive eyes
- clear catchlights
- rich iris detail
- emotional focus
- readable gaze direction
- intelligence and inner life

Avoid:

- tiny portrait eyes
- dead eyes
- glassy doll eyes
- exaggerated anime eyes
- mismatched eyes
- overrealistic uncanny eyes

The eyes should make children feel connection.

---

## Hair and Beards

Hair should feel tactile and real.

Use:

- individual strand detail
- soft volume
- historically appropriate styling
- age-appropriate color
- realistic gray, white, brown, black, or auburn tones
- gentle stylization for silhouette clarity

Avoid:

- painted hair
- marble hair
- engraving hatch marks
- melted hair
- overly perfect wig shapes
- modern hairstyles unless historically correct

Hair must support identity.

---

## Clothing

Clothing should be historically grounded but visually readable.

Use:

- period-specific garments
- simplified silhouettes
- visible fabric weave
- tactile folds
- rich but plausible colors
- readable collars, robes, coats, hats, armor, or tools

Avoid:

- random fantasy costumes
- generic robes for every scholar
- modern clothing unless appropriate
- excessive tiny details that confuse the silhouette
- clothing copied as brush strokes from paintings

The clothing should tell the child when and where the figure belongs.

---

## Props and Environments

Props and environments should reinforce identity.

Examples:

- Newton: prism, books, apple, study, telescope
- Ibn al-Haytham: optics tools, manuscripts, observatory elements
- Archimedes: geometric diagrams, water, mechanical devices
- Ada Lovelace: papers, early computing diagrams, elegant study
- Marie Curie: laboratory glassware, notebooks, glowing scientific atmosphere

Props should support story, not clutter the image.

---

## Facial Expression

Default canonical portraits should use expressions that are:

- intelligent
- authentic
- curious
- emotionally alive
- capable of focus, frustration, delight, or resolve
- specific to the person's emotional signature
- slightly heightened
- not exaggerated

Avoid:

- blank seriousness
- grim severity
- clownish smiling
- rage
- fear
- lifeless academic posing

The canonical image should feel like the character is about to think, speak, or discover.

It should not feel like a universal "Identity Forge expression" applied to every figure.

Newton's concentration, Archimedes' delight, Curie's resolve, and Tesla's restless imagination should require different facial choices.

---

## Composition

Use cinematic bust or half-body composition.

Preferred:

- 3/4 view
- face dominant
- shoulders visible
- historically meaningful background
- shallow depth of field
- clear silhouette
- strong eye contact or intentional gaze

Avoid:

- distant full-body images for canonical identity
- cluttered backgrounds
- profile-only unless historically necessary
- tiny faces
- flat centered passport-photo framing

---

## Final Art Direction Test

A successful Identity Forge image should answer yes to all:

- Does this look like a high-end animated film character?
- Does this preserve the historical figure?
- Would a child want to watch this character?
- Could this character stand beside the rest of the cast?
- Is the image free of old-source artifacts?
- Is the lighting cinematic and readable?
- Is the face emotionally alive?
- Is the expression specific to this historical person?

If not, refine.
