## Purpose

This document defines how Identity Forge stylizes human proportions.

The goal is to create characters who are expressive and appealing to children while preserving recognizable historical identity.

Stylization should enhance identity, not replace it.

---

## Core Principle

Identity Forge uses controlled stylization.

Every character begins with historical facial structure.

The system then gently adjusts proportion for cinematic readability and child appeal.

The result should feel like:

> a real historical person translated into an animated feature film character

not:

> a random cartoon loosely inspired by history

---

## Stylization Range

Identity Forge should operate in a moderate stylization range.

Too little stylization creates:

- stiff portraits
- textbook seriousness
- uncanny realism
- low child appeal

Too much stylization creates:

- caricature
- parody
- loss of identity
- childishness
- inconsistency

The correct range is:

Historical identity: 70–80%  
Animated stylization: 20–30%

---

## Face Shape

Preserve:

- skull shape
- jawline
- cheekbone structure
- chin shape
- forehead proportion
- recognizable facial asymmetry

May adjust:

- softness of planes
- friendliness of expression
- readability of silhouette
- harshness of wrinkles or shadows

Do not replace the face with a generic attractive animated face.

---

## Eyes

Eyes may be enlarged slightly for emotional readability.

Guideline:

- increase perceived expressiveness by 10–15%
- preserve eye spacing
- preserve eye shape
- preserve brow relationship
- preserve gaze character

Avoid:

- anime eyes
- doll eyes
- tiny portrait eyes
- mismatched eye scale across cast members
- over-bright magical eyes

The eyes must remain believable for the person.

---

## Nose

The nose is a major identity anchor.

Preserve:

- bridge shape
- width
- length
- tip structure
- nostril shape
- profile character

Do not beautify or genericize the nose.

Minor softening is allowed, but the historical nose should remain recognizable.

---

## Mouth

The mouth controls emotional readability.

Preserve:

- mouth width
- lip shape
- natural expression tendency
- age-appropriate structure

May adjust:

- subtle human emotion
- slight smile readiness
- readability of emotion

Avoid:

- giant cartoon smiles
- modern influencer expressions
- forced happiness
- flattened serious lips if emotional life is needed

---

## Brows

Brows are critical for personality.

They help separate quiet intensity, playful curiosity, calm wisdom, disciplined observation, and restless imagination.

Preserve:

- brow height
- brow weight
- brow angle
- relationship to eyes

May enhance:

- curiosity
- intensity
- concern
- wonder
- determination
- skepticism
- delight
- concentration

Avoid:

- villain brows
- clown brows
- random modern grooming
- excessive symmetry

---

## Head Size

A slight increase in head prominence is allowed for animated appeal.

Guideline:

- head may feel 5–8% more prominent than strict realism
- face remains anatomically coherent
- shoulders and clothing support the larger cinematic head

Avoid:

- toddler proportions
- bobblehead proportions
- inconsistent scale across characters

---

## Hands

If hands are visible, they may be slightly expressive.

Guideline:

- preserve adult anatomy
- allow clear gesture language
- avoid tiny stiff portrait hands
- avoid oversized comedy hands

Hands should support the character’s role:

- inventor hands
- scholar hands
- artist hands
- explorer hands
- scientist hands

---

## Body and Posture

Canonical portraits should preserve adult dignity.

Use:

- upright posture
- slight energy
- thoughtful lean
- role-specific body language
- readable silhouette

Avoid:

- slouching unless character-specific
- superhero exaggeration
- childish posture
- stiff statue posture
- lifeless portrait posture

---

## Age

Age must be respected.

Do not make every figure young.

However, age should be rendered with humanity and appeal.

Use:

- softened wrinkles
- expressive facial planes
- lively eyes
- healthy skin tone
- expressive posture

Avoid:

- harsh decrepitude
- corpse-like realism
- plastic age removal
- random youthification

The character should feel alive at their historical age.

---

## Silhouette

Every character should have a recognizable silhouette.

Silhouette may come from:

- hair
- hat
- beard
- robe
- collar
- tools
- posture
- shoulders
- accessories

A child should be able to identify major characters from shape alone after repeated exposure.

---

## Cast Consistency

Across the full cast:

- eye scale should remain compatible
- head scale should remain compatible
- texture realism should remain compatible
- stylization should remain compatible
- facial exaggeration should remain compatible

No character should look like they came from a different studio.

---

## Proportion Approval Checklist

Before approval, ask:

- Is the historical face still recognizable?
- Are the eyes expressive but not exaggerated?
- Is the nose preserved?
- Is the age respected?
- Is the character alive, authentic, and accessible to children?
- Does the proportion language support a distinct emotional identity?
- Does the silhouette read clearly?
- Does the character fit with the rest of the cast?
- Is the stylization consistent with Identity Forge?

If any answer is no, refine.
