# Purpose

Identity Forge does not create individual portraits.

It creates a cast.

Every historical figure must feel as though they belong in the same cinematic universe, regardless of their century, geography, culture, profession, or historical significance.

The purpose of this document is to define the rules that ensure every canonical character feels like they were designed by the same world-class animation studio, under the same art director, for the same feature film.

---

# Core Philosophy

The audience should never think:

> "These were generated separately."

Instead, they should instinctively believe:

> "These characters all belong together."

Every design decision should strengthen that illusion.

The cast is more important than any individual image.

---

# The One-Movie Test

Every canonical character must pass the One-Movie Test.

Imagine every approved historical figure standing together in a single promotional poster for an animated feature film.

Ask:

- Does anyone feel out of place?
- Does anyone appear from another animation studio?
- Does anyone appear more realistic than the others?
- Does anyone appear significantly more cartoonish?
- Does anyone use different lighting?
- Does anyone have incompatible proportions?

If the answer to any question is yes,

continuity has failed.

---

# Shared Visual Language

Every character shares the same visual language.

These elements should remain remarkably consistent across the cast.

## Lighting

Warm.

Soft.

Cinematic.

Volumetric.

Readable.

Never harsh.

Never documentary-flat.

---

## Materials

Skin should feel alive.

Hair should feel touchable.

Fabric should feel woven.

Wood should feel carved.

Metal should feel physical.

Everything should possess believable material qualities.

---

## Stylization

Every face exists within the same stylization range.

No character should suddenly become:

hyperreal

anime

comic book

low-poly

toy-like

photographic

Stylization should remain stable across the universe.

---

## Color

The cast should share one cinematic palette.

Warm.

Optimistic.

Rich.

Readable.

Historically grounded.

Children should recognize the universe before recognizing the individual.

---

# Character Scale

Characters should appear physically compatible.

Head proportions.

Eye proportions.

Hand size.

Shoulder width.

Overall anatomy.

Everything should suggest that these people inhabit the same physical world.

---

# Emotional Consistency

Identity Forge tells stories about curiosity.

Discovery.

Perseverance.

Creativity.

Wonder.

Risk.

Failure.

Breakthrough.

Therefore,

every character should remain emotionally readable and appropriate for children.

Continuity must not flatten struggle into pleasantness.

The cast should share a hopeful universe,

but each character should still feel capable of hard work, uncertainty, obsession, and earned discovery.

Different personalities are encouraged.

Different emotional intensity is encouraged.

Emotional inaccessibility is not.

No two characters should feel emotionally interchangeable.

Continuity means shared world language,

not shared temperament.

# Expression Language

Every character possesses a default emotional state.

Examples:

Newton

Obsessive concentration.

Archimedes

Explosive breakthrough.

Curie

Quietly unstoppable resolve.

Tesla

Restless invention.

Ada Lovelace

Elegant vision.

Ibn al-Haytham

Disciplined observation.

Einstein

Playful amusement.

Expressions may include concentration, uncertainty, frustration, or breakthrough.

The correct expression is the one that clarifies that character's emotional identity.

One shared default expression across the cast is a continuity failure.

Expressions should remain subtle.

Identity Forge avoids exaggerated cartoon acting in canonical portraits.

# Costume Consistency

Clothing should always communicate:

era

profession

culture

social status

identity

However,

costume complexity should remain visually balanced.

Visual complexity should remain consistent across the cast.

Canonical portraits should place characters within environments that feel like they exist in one coherent cinematic world.

Examples include:

libraries

workshops

gardens

studios

Each environment reflects the character,

material quality,

cinematography,

# Interaction Test

Every approved character should plausibly interact with every other approved character.

Imagine:

Archimedes meeting Marie Curie.

Ibn al-Haytham explaining optics to Leonardo.

Hypatia teaching Einstein.

The audience should never question whether these characters belong together visually.

# Continuity Review

Every new canonical character should be reviewed beside the existing cast.

Questions:

Does this character belong?

Is the stylization consistent?

Does the lighting match?

Are proportions compatible?

Would children immediately accept this character into the universe?

If uncertainty exists,

continue refinement.

---

# Cast Expansion

As the universe grows,

new characters should integrate seamlessly.

The fiftieth character should feel as though they were designed at the same time as the first.

The one hundredth character should strengthen the universe,

not dilute it.

Scalability is a core design objective.

---

# Common Failure Modes

Cast continuity has failed when:

One character appears hyper-realistic.

Another appears heavily cartooned.

Lighting differs dramatically.

Color philosophy changes.

Skin rendering changes.

Eye proportions drift.

Materials become inconsistent.

Historical figures resemble unrelated franchises.

Individual artistic experiments override the universe.

The audience notices the production process instead of the story.

---

# Cast Approval Checklist

Before adding a new canonical character ask:

✓ Does this character immediately feel like part of the Identity Forge Universe?

✓ Does the stylization match the existing cast?

✓ Is lighting consistent?

✓ Are proportions compatible?

✓ Does the color palette harmonize?

✓ Is emotional readability preserved?

✓ Is the emotional signature distinct from every existing character?

✓ Does the character feel capable of changing civilization?

✓ Would this character appear natural beside every existing character?

✓ Does this strengthen the universe?

If any answer is no,

continue refinement.

---

# Relationship to Director Desk

Identity Forge builds the cast.

Director Desk directs the cast.

Identity Forge defines who the characters are.

Director Desk determines what they do.

This separation guarantees long-term visual continuity across every production.

---

# Success Criteria

Cast Continuity succeeds when:

✓ Every historical figure belongs to one unified cinematic universe.

✓ No character appears visually isolated.

✓ The cast feels cohesive regardless of historical period.

✓ Children recognize the shared artistic language immediately.

✓ New characters integrate naturally.

✓ The universe scales from ten characters to hundreds without losing identity.

---

# Final Principle

Children do not fall in love with isolated portraits.

They fall in love with characters.

They remember friendships.

They remember conversations.

They imagine adventures.

They imagine attempts.

They imagine failures.

They imagine breakthroughs.

Identity Forge therefore creates more than historical reconstructions.

It creates a family of remarkable people who never lived in the same century—

yet feel as though they have always belonged together.

That shared sense of belonging is the true measure of Cast Continuity.
